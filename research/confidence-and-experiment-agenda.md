# Confidence assessment and experimental agenda

Status: **Provisional research judgment, 2026-10-10.** This assesses the six dossiers; it is not a seventh feature proposal, an implementation plan, or an experiment report. No new model experiments were run. The operator asked for candid prioritization and for the agent to take responsibility for research direction rather than mirror the operator's preferences.

## Central assessment

The strongest near-term candidates are faithful compact views and preserving contextual applicability. The most interesting less-certain candidate is reusable methods with explicit conditions and remaining work. The weakest current case is for full simplicial, globular, higher-type, or stack machinery: the dossiers have not specified an indispensable operation that beats ordinary records, references, and small checks.

This is confidence in where to investigate, not measured probability of success. Structural correctness, faithful interpretation, agent behavior, and net operational value are four different claims. No candidate has yet established all four. Confidence that any additional mathematical layer will beat its strongest ordinary comparator is currently low across all six. The independent critique was more pessimistic about incremental benefit than the initial ranking; the ranking below concerns research priority, not readiness to ship.

We also lack a representative failure corpus showing how often these problems occur in ordinary Circadian use. Plausible counterexamples are not a prevalence estimate. A candidate may be coherent and solve its synthetic example while addressing an uncommon or already adequately handled situation.

The research risks either decorating ordinary engineering with mathematical names or reducing every unfamiliar idea to ordinary engineering before examining its distinctive operation. Preserve a bounded research lane for a genuinely different operation, but require its definition, an information-loss example, and a comparison against the strongest simpler representation. Fringe origin earns investigation, not exemption from evidence.

The agent should own hypotheses, protocol choices, counterexamples, and recommendations to retire work. Its self-description of continuity or preference for a representation is not an acceptance test. Relevant outcomes include faithful recollection, appropriate carry-over, successful correction, usable context, and preserving the human accounts entrusted to the system.

## Assessment of each proposal

| Proposal | Confidence in the research opportunity, not measured benefit | What would change the assessment |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Coordinated views | Highest of the six; incremental benefit remains unmeasured | Under constrained context, preserve qualifications and useful recall better than ordinary compact presentation without excessive expansion calls |
| 2. Contextual compatibility | Moderately high for explicit applicability; lower for general semantic comparison | Reduce both false conflicts and missed conflicts on raw conversations, including unknown applicability and subjective accounts |
| 3. Typed composition | Moderate for narrow attribution and source distinctions; low for a universal ontology | Show fewer unsupported conclusions beyond the benefit of giving the baseline the same explicit information |
| 4. Multi-input operations | Moderate, with substantial potential and substantial selection/invalidation risk | A fresh session reuses a method on changed inputs more effectively than recalling a short prose recipe or solving directly |
| 5. Higher-order relations | Plausible for joint event/role records; low for full higher structure | Distinguish events and interpretations better than whole episodes or ordinary event records; identify an operation requiring additional laws |
| 6. Checked transformations | High confidence in checking specified fields; low confidence this alone improves semantic fidelity | Catch consequential end-to-end distortions beyond basic validation without granting misleading assurance to free prose |

### 1. Coordinated views

This has the clearest experimentally accessible question: can a fresh agent orient with less irrelevant material while retaining what matters? The weakness is task selection and semantic loss. A compact answer can hide its own omissions; repeated expansion can spend more than the initial compression saves. Selecting by the current task must not be assumed available at startup.

Compare ordinary recall, a carefully written compact baseline, and the preservation-aware view. Give equal evidence and compare both matched delivery budgets and actual total consumption. Include no-use cases, an out-of-scope high-ranked record, an important qualification near the cutoff, and a later question requiring expansion. If ordinary presentation provides equivalent fidelity and cost within a declared useful margin, retain the simpler presentation.

### 2. Contextual compatibility

The valuable distinction is origin versus applicability: where an account was recorded need not determine where it holds. Different activities, times, or speakers can explain apparent disagreement. However, extracting those distinctions is often the hard work; a correct solver cannot repair an incorrect interpretation.

Use matched histories that change one condition: person, activity, time, uncertainty, or attribution. Some pairs should coexist, some should conflict, and some should remain unknown. Add one small multi-record constraint case. Run first with reviewed annotations, then with raw conversations and proposed machine annotations. A gold-annotation success followed by extraction failure locates the bottleneck; it does not establish product readiness. Penalize answering unknown indiscriminately as well as unjustified reconciliation.

### 3. Typed composition

Narrow distinctions such as speaker versus subject or recording location versus event location seem worth testing. Broadly formalizing personal meaning looks much less promising. A well-typed wrong attribution is still wrong.

Compare identical substantive information expressed as clear prose, ordinary typed records, and records with path checks. Deliberately introduce a structurally valid wrong attribution and an unsupported inference from reported preference to enduring trait. Require both rejection of unsupported conclusions and retention of useful supported answers. This may become a few checks shared with proposals 1 and 6 rather than a separate extension.

### 4. Multi-input operations

This has the greatest exploratory appeal: memory could preserve a reusable way of combining evidence and what remains unresolved. The mnemonic analogy is useful as a hypothesis about an external aid's changing utility. It is not evidence of model-weight learning or a human-like internal memory process.

The central risks are discovering the method, deciding when it applies, and invalidating bound assumptions. Test a sequence: unfamiliar situation, repeated use with changed inputs, a changed condition, and a later irrelevant task. Compare direct solving, a concise prose recipe, and the structured method. Include both an explicitly supplied method and a discoverable method, charging creation and lookup costs. Remove or retire the aid when appropriate without treating the underlying meaningful memory as disposable. If the supplied method helps but discovering it costs more than it saves, selection is the problem to investigate.

### 5. Higher-order relations

The joint-experience argument is sound as a representational concern. It is not yet a strong argument for additional topology. Whole narrative episodes already retain much of the desired information, and ordinary event nodes can encode incidence.

Test separate pairwise meetings versus a shared meeting, repeated gatherings with changed roles, people mentioned but absent, and changed interpretations of one episode. Compare narrative, ordinary event records, and any proposed higher operation. Do not construct the control by deliberately deleting information that Circadian already preserves. If no operation beyond event/role references is specified, there is no meaningful higher-structure arm to run. Retaining the original account may be the entire useful result.

### 6. Checked transformations

It is feasible in principle to compare explicitly represented fields against a stated rule; the dossier contains no implementation evidence. The open question is how often such checks address actual memory damage and whether their premises are faithful.

Introduce dropped qualifications, changed subjects, altered candidate bytes, stale sources, and correct metadata accompanying misleading prose. Separate false acceptance from coverage: a checker that passes only trivial rewrites may be correct but of little value. Compare ordinary diff/provenance, field validation, and retained check receipts. If receipts do not improve recovery or later interpretation beyond recomputation, omit them. Narrative review must be able to reject a formal pass.

## The laboratory: a protocol and small runner, not a new platform

Use the existing runtime, plain fixture files, a manifest, raw result artifacts, and a report. Keep live memory and scheduled processes outside the experiment. No new database, daemon, dashboard, or orchestration service is justified. Reuse the same experimental skeleton across proposals without building an extension architecture first.

### Stage 1: establish discriminating cases without inference

Write a proposed pilot of 24 short histories across everyday relationships, travel, learning, creative work, practical planning, and technical work. This is a design choice, not a statistically justified sample size. Include positive, negative, unknown, and irrelevant cases. Explicitly protect rare meaningful events with no later productivity payoff.

Use counterfactual variants differing in one decisive detail, plus paraphrases that should not change the answer. Keep all variants of a history in the same development or held-out split. Define source facts, acceptable ambiguity, prohibited inferences, and the specific check. Future questions and expected answers must not enter capture/consolidation inputs. Synthetic checks can falsify a mechanism; passing them does not demonstrate value in ordinary use.

### Stage 2: isolate where a benefit or failure arises

Separate representation, extraction, and selection with four diagnostic conditions:

1. Reviewed representation, relevant material explicitly supplied: can the consumer use the information?
2. Machine-extracted representation, relevant material explicitly supplied: what does interpretation/extraction lose?
3. Reviewed representation, ordinary discovery: what does retrieval and selection lose?
4. Machine-extracted representation, ordinary discovery: do the components work together?

The subsequent lifecycle test starts from raw conversations and includes capture, consolidation, discovery, and a fresh consumer. It measures the whole system after these narrower diagnostics.

These are diagnostics, not necessarily a monotonic performance ordering. Hand-reviewed metadata is additional supervision and its cost must be visible. Information supplied only to the treatment invalidates a claim about the superior representation.

Core comparisons are current Circadian, the strongest simple equivalent, and the candidate. A no-memory control can diagnose whether the case needs memory at all. Directly supplying relevant source passages can diagnose downstream reasoning, but is not a guaranteed upper bound. Remove individual components to identify what causes any gain. Do not combine all six proposals initially.

### Stage 3: model selection and reproducibility

Select exact available model identifiers only after inspecting the local and remote endpoints. The operator has described those capabilities; this assessment does not certify current deployment or select a model by guesswork.

Initially fix one consumer to avoid multiplying variables. If a representation shows promise, test a second consumer and cross producer/consumer identities: A writes/B reads and B writes/A reads. A shared substrate should not require the originating model's private shorthand. A classifier is an optional candidate annotator or selector, tested separately from the consumer and never treated as a semantic oracle.

Every run needs an immutable manifest recording code and fixture hashes, prompts, model/provider and exposed revision, settings, tool schemas, context limits, source ordering, requested repetitions, scoring, and run failures. Record actual requests/responses, model-reported identity where available, stage timings, token consumption, tool calls, annotations, and memory artifacts. When a provider does not expose a stable revision, record that limitation. A failed request remains in the accounting; no silent retries that select convenient outputs.

Use paired trials and randomized order. Repetitions on one history are not independent histories. Use a pilot to estimate variability, then freeze a confirmatory protocol with minimum worthwhile benefit, tolerated regressions, stopping rules, and an untouched test split. Wide uncertainty means inconclusive, not equivalent. Report fidelity, coverage, time, context, inference expense, and authoring burden separately rather than hiding tradeoffs in one score.

Mechanical checks should handle mechanically decidable properties. For narrative fidelity, use a predeclared rubric, blinded assessment, a human-reviewed subset, and visible disagreements. A different judging model is not automatically an independent source of truth. Do not reward verbosity, blanket abstention, or novelty alone.

### Stage 4: test continuity, not just recall quizzes

Replay multi-session histories with fresh contexts and actual isolated lifecycle operations. Advance a controlled clock where applicable; inspect every intermediate memory artifact. Run diagnostic probes on cloned states so testing cannot inadvertently reinforce later checkpoints. Simulated elapsed time tests lifecycle logic, not months of real use. Evaluate appropriate unsolicited use and intrusive irrelevant recall, a correction, a legitimate change of preference, distraction, and later recollection. Include a rare meaningful episode and an auxiliary aid whose usefulness ends.

Probe repeated transformation: can a weak interpretation become an apparent fact after several capture/consolidation cycles? Keep original evidence distinct from model-derived interpretations and repeated citations to the same source. Test whether an agent can recover uncertainty and revise a prior conclusion without requiring the person to repeat the whole history.

After synthetic controls, use consented, separately handled naturalistic histories not used to design the representation. Unchanged replay measures behavior against a fixed history; it cannot establish how memory changes a live relationship. A later bounded prospective study would be needed for that stronger claim. General-purpose benefit requires separate reporting by domain, not merely an overall score dominated by technical tasks.

## External evaluation leads

LongMemEval explicitly covers extraction, cross-session reasoning, temporal reasoning, updates, and abstention. Its existing tasks can inform coverage and provide an external reference after fixing an exact dataset revision; they do not alone define Circadian's purpose. [Official LongMemEval repository](https://github.com/xiaowu0162/LongMemEval), inspected 2026-10-10.

LoCoMo evaluates extended conversations using questions, event summaries, and dialogue generation. [Original ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.747/), inspected 2026-10-10.

LoCoMo-Plus specifically targets applying latent constraints when a later cue differs semantically from the original disclosure. Its abstract motivates examining behavior beyond explicit recall questions. This is an evaluation lead, not an endorsement based on reproducing its results. [ACL paper](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1150/), inspected 2026-10-10.

## Recommended order and decision rules

1. Specify the small laboratory protocol and adversarial fixture set. Do not build six extensions.
2. First behavioral pilot: proposal 1, with qualification preservation as the narrow test and ordinary compact presentation as the strong baseline. Checks suggested by 3 and 6 can be evaluated individually, not bundled into an unidentifiable win.
3. Test proposal 2 independently, with gold annotations and raw-source extraction separated.
4. Reserve a bounded exploratory experiment for proposal 4. Its upside warrants investigation despite less confidence in immediate payoff.
5. Test ordinary event incidence for proposal 5 only when a concrete baseline failure motivates it. Keep richer structures in research until a distinctive operation exists.
6. Evaluate combinations only after individual contributions and costs are understood.

Stop a candidate arm for violated declared invariants or misleading semantic certification. Fix or retire it before broadening. Do not recommend integration merely because a pilot average improved. Require reproducible gains or an explicitly valued fidelity benefit, acceptable costs, no material concealed regressions, and a clear account of where it fails. Retain the simpler mechanism when it achieves the needed result.

For a bounded mathematical investigation, proposal 2's locally compatible but jointly inconsistent constraints is the clearest starting point: specify several finite cases and their alignment assumptions, then compare the insight and outputs with ordinary joins or enumeration. A new useful diagnostic would justify continuing. Merely recovering the same answer under categorical terminology would not. This theory exercise is separate from the first behavioral pilot and does not require a solver package.

The current outcome is a recommendation to investigate. No model has been selected, no experiment has been run, and no plugin architecture has been chosen.
