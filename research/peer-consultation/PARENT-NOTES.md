The consultation therefore gives us a useful separation:

- **Laboratory finding:** what a particular tested arrangement did.
- **Production assurance:** whether the deployed lifecycle has a consequential failure.
- **Capability exploration:** whether a new operation would offer something useful beyond today's behavior.

Our mistake was letting a sequence of laboratory questions silently take over the other two.

## The candidate worth retaining from this exchange

The peer's revised focus is applicability and retirement by condition, connecting proposals 02 and 04 with the original temporary mnemonic. An aid could cease to be useful because a situation changed even if it was used frequently. Conversely, a rare but meaningful memory may deserve preservation while being absent from today's wake context.

A plain example is a temporary navigation aid: useful while learning a place, unnecessary once navigation is familiar, and irrelevant to current orientation after moving away. Retiring the aid from active use should not erase the meaningful episode of learning to live there.

This is a conceptual candidate, not a demonstrated feature gap or a selected extension. No field, plugin, scheduler, model or storage format has been chosen.

## Reservations that remain after the clarification

1. **Expressibility is not the same as reliable behavior.** An ordinary note can literally say when it applies or expires. The peer's claim that a saved note necessarily cannot do this is too strong, especially when its proposed fields also contain plain language interpreted by an agent. A dedicated representation would need to improve recognition, selection, retirement or maintenance enough to justify itself. Merely naming two fields proves none of that.
2. **Rewriting NOW does not prove it cannot preserve a condition across weeks.** Production SLEEP receives the previous NOW as an input, as already documented in the dossier. Whether a condition survives is a behavioral question, not an impossibility implied by rewriting the file.
3. **Literal provenance is a narrow check.** The peer correctly withdrew its claim that quote validation was a semantic filter. Its remaining phrase that forged text cannot enter an atom still needs qualification: matching a quote to an episode does not verify the episode against the native transcript. A mistaken claim, speaker or condition can accompany matching text. We have not run a new audit of that production path.
4. **No measured superiority among the six proposals.** The peer's preference for one combined idea and shelving the others is architectural judgment from reading, not comparative evidence. The earlier dossiers remain provisional and preserved; none was deleted or declared disproven.
5. **The proposed audit thresholds are heuristics.** Twenty-five episodes and three occurrences are not validated acceptance criteria. The peer acknowledged sampling and severity matter. No such audit has been authorized by this consultation or executed.
6. **Corpus totals and history are unverified here.** The peer reported glob matches and explicitly did not open the episode contents. Those counts do not establish that every match is a unique production episode/atom, its writer provenance or its age. The parent did not turn them into a deployment claim.

These reservations do not make the consultation unhelpful. They are the distinctions that prevent outside advice from becoming another unquestioned program.
