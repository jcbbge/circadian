**01 — Views.** Accept all four decisions. The contract is precise enough once one property is added: under a fixed stable order, the view is budget-monotone. Growing the budget must only add records, never swap them. That is what separates select-then-truncate from re-ranking per budget, and it is checkable without any semantic input. Remaining change: state that the coverage footer is reserved before records and that a footer-only output is legal when no stub fits, so the view never emits a bare claim to fill space. Additional test V13: same population and order, budgets b1 < b2 < b3. Expected: includedRefs(b1) ⊆ includedRefs(b2) ⊆ includedRefs(b3), omittedCount non-increasing, and a disagreement marker present at b2 remains present at b3. An implementation that reorders by budget fails. No discriminating case remains that would diagnose the default; V02's comparator is a deliberately different operation, not a claimed defect.

**02 — Compatibility.** Accept the separate alignment input, the explicit-domain joint checker, the eight-variable cap fixture, and the four-way distinction among known, irrelevant, unrestricted and unknown. The grouped report avoids a global verdict provided grouping is defined: a group is a connected component of the reviewed alignment relation, and a record with unknown alignment to everything is a singleton group with verdict unknown. That definition is the remaining change. Additional test C14: three records where r1 and r2 are aligned and conflict, and r3 has unknown alignment to both. Expected: two groups, {r1, r2} conflict and {r3} unknown; r3's presence changes nothing in the first group's verdict, witness or assignments examined. A checker that merges on shared spelling, or that lets an unknown record block the aligned group, fails. The joint enumerator is ordinary bounded code; the sheaf contribution is only the question it asks.

**03 — Typed relations.** I accept the correction and withdraw the two-self-report threshold. Two sourced reports on distinct occasions are two reports; occasion count is not evidence of duration, and the operation should return the grouped reports with their occasions and stop. I also withdraw my naive name-join foil; the comparator must be a competent typed join, as T02 and T10 now specify. Agreed that atom claims may mention other people, that multiple speakers use an attribution relation, and that recording location never equates to occasion setting. The one equation worth keeping is T04. Remaining change: make the reviewed identity mapping an explicit input to the join, so speaker-equals-subject is decided by that mapping and never by string equality. Additional test T14: R5 has speaker "M." and subject "Mira." With a reviewed alias M. = Mira, R5 joins the by-and-about intersection as a self-report. Without the alias, R5 appears as unresolved identity, not as hearsay and not as self-report. This is composition doing work: the self-report set is an intersection of two declared paths under a supplied identity, which no single relation yields.

**04 — Operations.** Accept all four corrections: ledger status is not continuing applicability; pure instantiation returns a proposed observation and persists nothing; three known pure functions, explicit `asOf`, typed slots and the residual property replace any combinator tree. With three fixed functions the composition is ordinary code, and I should say so plainly: the mathematics is implemented by function application, and the testable addition is the aid record, meaning the bindings and remaining slot names as data. R06, R07 and R08 do exercise substitution and partial evaluation without an interpreter. Remaining change: define the residual as the aid record itself, not a generated function, so R06 compares `instantiate(aid, rest)` to `plan(all)`. Additional test R15: two aids over the same plan, one binding preferences and one binding availability. Each residual applied to its complement must equal the full plan and equal the other. Expected: identical result and identical deduplicated evidence set. This checks that specialization commutes with slot choice, which is the partial-evaluation claim rather than a renamed closure.

**05 — Joint experiences.** Accept all: mention does not imply absence, co-attendance at a long event does not establish simultaneity, question identity is a reviewed local ID, plain typed links with the same endpoint check are an acceptable implementation, and reverse-link maintenance is a counted cost. The intended benefit survives: H05 for anchorless co-retrieval and H06/H07 for boundary rejection. The underspecified case is authorship. The checked boundary is the pair (resolved occasion, local question); the author is a label on the interpretation and not part of the boundary. Additional test H16: I4 is authored by Dana about D1/Q1, Dana having been told about the evening later. Expected: `checkRevisit(I1, I4)` returns sameBoundary; `recallOccasion(D1)` shows both accounts labelled by author with "author not an evidenced participant" on I4; Dana's attendance stays unknown. This separates interpretation from participation and keeps a non-attendee's reading legitimate without inventing presence.

**06 — Transformations.** Accept the correction: an unannotated field on either side is notChecked, and only an explicitly absent field against a known value fails. My algorithm conflated the two; K07 is right. Accept that only exact fixed-render recomputation is structurally checkable and that arbitrary prose stays unassessed with matching metadata. Accept the receipt limits. Is binding and limited equivalence meaningful beyond ordinary validation? Modestly. Binding distinguishes "this result concerns these exact bytes, annotations and rule" from "a check ran"; a well-built B may provide that, and equivalence is fine. K14 is a field-preservation property of the fixed renderer, which is ordinary property testing. The receipt's separate benefit is history, not validity: after inputs are gone, it can say a check ran, on which fields, with which result, without reproducing content. Additional test K18: make a receipt for a modality failure, then erase the source. Expected: `verifyApplicability` returns unavailable; the receipt still reports `fail(modality)` labelled historical; it contains digests and field names only, and no field values or quotes. A receipt storing values fails.
