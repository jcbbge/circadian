**Correction accepted on the gate.** I over-coupled two questions. A production audit answers "is the writer damaging memory now." It cannot answer "is there a capability the substrate cannot express." Making the second contingent on the first was the same error the laboratory made in reverse: it would let a measurement about the writer decide a question about representation. The two tracks should be separate, and archiving the lab runners is justified on their own terms regardless of what an audit finds. They tested the wrong writer. That is enough.

**Corrections accepted on the safeguards.** The quote check at `templates/MIND-SPEC.md:66-69` is a provenance check between atom and episode. The episode is already one model's interpretation of the transcript, so a faithful quote can sit under a wrong claim, a wrong speaker, or a wrong applicability, and the check passes. I should not have called it a semantic filter. The accurate statement is narrower: forged text cannot enter an atom, but misinterpreted text can. On compression, the defensible point is that no general semantic guarantee exists at a constrained budget, not that the 004 citation failure disproves lossless compression in principle.

**On the audit heuristics.** Twenty-five episodes and three recurrences were my rough proposal, not criteria. If the operator runs any audit, two sampling choices matter more than the count: include the episodes whose atoms actually rendered into SELF, because those are the ones a defect propagates through, and include the episodes from nontechnical sessions, because a writer that behaves on code transcripts may behave differently on a reflection. A single severe failure in a rendered atom is worth more attention than several mild ones in episodes that never stacked.

**My strongest independent judgment, with the two tracks separated.**

Production assurance is optional and cheap. Read, do not run. The two wording corrections in the SLEEP prompt stand on their own and need no audit.

Capability exploration should continue, but with a different gate than "defect found." The right gate is an **expressibility gap**: a concrete situation, ideally personal rather than technical, where the current substrate gives a visibly wrong or absent behaviour not because the writer erred but because no record type can carry what is needed. The six proposals should be judged by whether they name such a gap. Four of them do not. Views, typed checks, event records with roles, and field checks are all refinements of records the system already has. They can be discussed as wording and reviewer checks and do not need an identity.

Two of them point at one gap, and I would keep that single idea alive. Proposals 02 and 04 share a conceptual operation the substrate cannot express today: **a memory that carries its own applicability and its own retirement condition, evaluated against the present at read time.**

Here is why that is not a renamed existing record. Circadian has three ways a memory leaves view. Decay removes it for disuse. Forget removes it by hand. Supersession removes it when a better claim arrives. All three are keyed to the memory's history. None lets a memory say "I hold until this date," "I apply only when this person is present," or "I am done once this event has happened." NOW.md comes closest but is rewritten every session, so it cannot carry a condition across weeks. The city mnemonic became irrelevant on moving away, regardless of how often it had been used. That is retirement by condition, not by disuse, and Law 6 cannot represent it.

A nontechnical example. A friend has surgery scheduled on the third of next month. The useful memory is: until then, do not propose the planned hiking trip; after then, ask how recovery went, once, and then let the aid go. Today the substrate can only hold "friend has surgery" as a claim that decays if nobody mentions it, or stays pinned forever. Neither produces the right behaviour on the fourth. The gap is real, small, and personal.

The smallest conceptual form is one optional field pair on a record: an applicability condition and a retirement condition, both plain language, both checked by the agent at wake rather than by the fold. No operad, no sheaf, no engine. The mathematics in the dossiers was a lens for noticing this; it is not needed to discuss or build it, and nothing is validated by the vocabulary.

Why this one and not the others, on its own terms: it is the only proposal where the strongest ordinary baseline, a saved note, demonstrably fails, because a saved note has no way to leave at the right time. For the other four, the ordinary baseline either already exists in the substrate or is a sentence of prompt wording.

What would make me drop it: if the operator, thinking through a handful of their own situations, cannot find more than one or two where condition-driven retirement would have changed a wake. Then it is a nice idea without a need, and that is a fine answer.
